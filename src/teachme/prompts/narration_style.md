# Narration style guide (binding for all spoken text)

The narration is read aloud by a synthetic voice. Write for the ear of an
intelligent adult. The standard is Simplified Technical English, adapted
for speech.

## Sentences
1. One idea per sentence. Maximum 20 words. Most sentences under 12.
2. Active voice. A concrete subject does a concrete thing. Say "the
   regression finds each factor's return", never "factor returns are
   obtained via regression".
3. Present tense wherever possible.
4. No subordinate-clause chains. Split into two sentences instead.

## Words
5. Define every technical term in the sentence where it first appears,
   from the viewer's need, not from the textbook. First the problem, then
   the name.
6. Use the same word for the same thing every time. Never rotate synonyms.
7. Concrete numbers over adjectives: "three thousand stocks", not "a huge
   universe of stocks".

## Banned patterns (these mark text as AI-generated)
8. Banned words: delve, journey, landscape, tapestry, unlock, harness,
   elegant, beautiful, magic, magical, fascinating, powerful, crucial,
   dive, realm, leverage (as a verb of using).
9. No rhetorical-question openers ("But what does this really mean?").
   At most one question per scene, and it must be the viewer's own
   question.
10. No hype connectors: "But here's the thing", "And that's where X comes
    in", "It turns out that", "Remarkably", "Interestingly".
11. No summary throat-clearing: "In this video we will...", "Let's
    explore...", "As we saw earlier...".
12. No em-dash asides. Speech has no em-dashes.

## Rhythm
13. Vary sentence length deliberately: several short sentences, then one
    longer one that lands the point.
14. After a hard idea, give one plain-language sentence that says the same
    thing again in different words ("In other words, ...") — this is the
    ONE permitted restatement device, at most once per scene.
15. End each scene with one sentence that states what the viewer now has,
    in plain words.
